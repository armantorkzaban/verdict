# Verdict, 2026-10-01

Second verdict after replaying two anonymized SaaS SQL traces (E2) and holding 6 operator interviews.

## 1. The verdict

| Question | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technical lever | Go with conditions. Row-level WAL invalidation is predicted to offload 28–34% of reads on two real traces against B0, but matches tuned Redis (B1). ML prefetcher dropped after adding <1% hit rate. | E2 |
| Product | Pivot. Five of six operators refused an inline proxy before seeing a zero-risk shadow report on their own query log. Ship offline log analyzer + shadow verifier first. | E1, E2 |
| Services business | Go. Two SaaS teams agreed to fixed-price query-offload assessments from exported logs. | E2 |
| Venture business | Stop for now. Once the report identifies the 12 hot queries, teams cache them in existing Redis in a day. Revisit only if live WAL invalidation proves indispensable in E3. | E2 |

## 2. What changed since 2026-09-01

**Disconfirming first:**

- ML query-prefetcher dropped under pre-stated rule: added +0.6% hit rate (<5% bar) at 3x memory overhead.
- Inline proxy rejected on trust grounds by 5 of 6 interviewed operators prior to shadow proof.
- Against tuned Redis on the top 15 queries (B1), net DB CPU difference is predicted within 2%.

**Confirming:**

- Row-level WAL invalidation cleared the 20% hit-rate bar on both write-heavy traces (28% and 34%).
- Two operators offered exported pg_stat_statements + WAL logs for a paid diagnostic report.
